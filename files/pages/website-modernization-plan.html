    Summary

    The DCFC presence online today is one page on the diocesan website. It holds the Commission’s preamble and six objectives, two contacts, a Simbang Gabi 2025 schedule, a photo gallery, an empty “Videos” section, and a contact form. Nothing on it covers the Commission’s year-round events, parish Filipino communities, or the planning tools DCFC has already built, such as the feast-day plan, brochure, missal, speaker guides, and table signs.

    This plan describes how to turn that page into a useful, well-run site. The site would explain who DCFC is and what it is for, show what is happening and where, and give parishes and ministry leaders the forms, templates, and guidance they need. Because we do not yet know who maintains the diocesan site, how it is hosted, or what it costs, the plan starts with a short discovery phase to answer those questions before anything is built.

    Two Paths Compared

    Plan A Bottom-up pilot

    Modernize DCFC first, then share the template

    Rebuild the Filipino Community section as a pilot within diocesan standards. Package it as a reusable template, offer it to the other 17 cultural communities, and use the results to make the case for a diocese-wide refresh.

    About 6–9 months to launch · mostly volunteer effort with modest costs · DCFC controls the pace

    Plan B Top-down redesign

    Modernize the whole diocesan site at once

    Propose a full redesign of sdcatholic.org led by the Chancery and Communications, with a hired agency. It would cover offices, parishes, schools, and all cultural communities together, with DCFC as one stakeholder.

    About 12–24 months · agency budget and Chancery approval required · diocese controls the pace

    Recommendation Pursue Plan A, built so it can grow into Plan B. It is feasible now, delivers value to Filipino families within a year, needs no large budget during the diocese’s Chapter 11 reorganization, and gives the diocese a tested, low-risk model to adopt. If discovery shows the diocese already has a redesign planned or funded, switch to Plan B and join it as the pilot community. The comparison is in Section 7.

    Gaps Against What the Community Needs

    • No events calendar. Major celebrations such as the San Pedro Calungsod Feast and Simbang Gabi are missing or out of date.
    • No resource library. The plans, brochures, missals, liturgy planning sheets, and signs DCFC already produces are not shared.
    • No map of Filipino communities. Families cannot find which parishes have Filipino ministries, Masses, or choirs.
    • No youth or young adult content, even though San Pedro Calungsod is the patron of Filipino youth.
    • No news, stories, or sign-ups. There is no way to subscribe, volunteer, or register for an event.
    • The page is hard to find. It sits three levels deep under Cultural Diversity, with no short address such as sdcatholic.org/filipino.
    • Language. The page has English and Spanish, but no Tagalog or Cebuano for elders and new arrivals.
    • Accessibility. Images lack alt text, menus are duplicated for screen readers, and required fields are marked only with asterisks.

    What We Don’t Know Yet

    Discovery in Phase 0 has to answer these questions. They decide which plan is feasible and what it costs.

    • Who maintains sdcatholic.org: Communications staff, Diocesan IT, or an outside agency, and under what contract?
    • Where is the site hosted, who holds the domain and DNS, and who has administrator access?
    • Can a ministry volunteer receive an Editor login for one section, or must every change go through staff?
    • Does the diocese have brand guidelines, a web policy, or a social media and photo policy for ministries?
    • Is a redesign of the diocesan site already planned, budgeted, or out for bids?
    • How do Chapter 11 reorganization rules affect new vendor contracts or spending?
    • Which of the other 17 cultural communities have active leaders who would want a better page?
    • Who in DCFC can commit 3–5 hours a week to keep content current after launch?

    Site ownerOwns sdcatholic.org: hosting, domain, security, backups, brand standards, final publish rightsDiocesan Communications / IT

    Governance Rules That Keep the Site Alive

    • Diocese owns accounts and domains, never an individual. Use role emails such as dcfc@… or dcfc.president@… that pass from board to board.
    • Publishing workflow: an editor drafts, the web lead reviews, the pastoral reviewer checks faith content, and the site owner publishes when required.
    • Service levels: event updates live within 5 business days; contact-form replies within 3 business days.
    • Content calendar: monthly events refresh, quarterly review of resources and contacts, and an annual leadership update after each election.
    • Handover document: logins (in a shared password manager), procedures, and vendor contacts, so a change in volunteers does not stall the site.
    • Sunset rule: anything older than 12 months moves to an archive, so nothing like the empty “Videos” section stays up.
    Section 4

    Hosting & Platform Options

    Starting from the infrastructure the diocese already runs

    The current page appears to run on the diocese’s WordPress site, with an English/Spanish plugin and an events calendar plugin. That is a reasonable base: WordPress is widely supported, easy for volunteers to edit, and already paid for. The options below run from most integrated to most independent.

    OptionHow it worksEst. cost / yrBest when…
    1. Section of sdcatholic.org PreferredRebuild the Filipino Community page as a small set of child pages in the same WordPress install, with a reusable page template or block patterns and a short address such as sdcatholic.org/filipino.$0 hosting; volunteer or vendor timeThe diocese grants section-level Editor access or a responsive staff contact
    2. Diocesan sub-siteA WordPress Multisite sub-site, such as filipino.sdcatholic.org, on diocesan hosting with the diocesan theme. DCFC administers only its own sub-site.$0–$500 added hosting; setup by vendorThe diocese wants communities to self-manage while keeping central control
    3. Separate DCFC siteDCFC’s own domain on managed WordPress hosting or a site builder such as Squarespace or Wix, linked from the diocesan page.$200–$600 plus domain (~$20)The diocese cannot give access, and DCFC needs to move fast
    4. Static siteA site generated from Markdown files and hosted free on a static host, with forms through a form service.$0–$100A technical volunteer will maintain it long term (otherwise not advised)

    Costs are rough market ranges for planning only and should be confirmed with quotes. Ask vendors about nonprofit and religious-organization pricing.

    Decision Guide

    Choose Option 1 if…

    • Communications agrees to an Editor role or a named publisher
    • The diocesan theme can support cards, calendars, and downloads
    • We want the change to carry over to other communities (Plan A)

    Choose Option 2 if…

    • Several communities want their own space
    • IT is comfortable running Multisite
    • The diocese wants a model for all 18 communities

    Use Option 3 only as a bridge

    • Register the domain to DCFC (or the diocese), not a person
    • Follow diocesan brand rules and link back to sdcatholic.org
    • Plan to merge back into the diocesan site later

    Technical Requirements for Any Option

    • Security: HTTPS, automatic core and plugin updates, two-factor login for every editor, and the lowest access role that works
    • Backups: daily, kept off-site, with a tested restore at launch and once a year
    • Performance: optimized images (WebP), loads in under 3 seconds on a phone, cached pages
    • Forms: spam protection, routing to a role inbox, no sensitive data collected, and a written retention period
    • Calendar: use the diocesan events plugin, filtered to a “Filipino” category, so events also appear in the main diocesan calendar
    • Search & sharing: clean addresses, page titles and descriptions, social preview images, and a Google Business-style listing for DCFC
    • Analytics: privacy-respecting page counts to see what people use, such as downloads and the most-viewed events
    • Documentation: a one-page “how to update the site” guide for every editor
    Section 5 · Plan A

    Bottom-Up: DCFC Pilot, Then Expand the Template

    Prove it with one community, then share it with all of them

    ApproachDCFC builds a modern Filipino Community section inside the diocesan site (Hosting Option 1 or 2), designed as a reusable template. After launch, the template is packaged with a how-to guide and offered to the other 17 cultural communities, followed by a proposal to apply the same design system diocese-wide.

    Phases & Timeline

    Phase 0 · DiscoveryNov – Dec 2026
    Identify the site owner and vendor, confirm access and policies, name the DCFC web lead and editors, and agree on the scope and short address with Communications. Output: signed-off scope and roles.
    Phase 1 · ContentDec 2026 – Feb 2027
    Rewrite the mission in plain language; gather leadership bios, the parish community directory, the 2027 event list, and the resource library; collect photos with consent; arrange Spanish and Tagalog translation. Output: approved content in a shared folder.
    Phase 2 · DesignJan – Feb 2027
    Design wireframes for Home, About, Events, Communities, and Resources inside diocesan brand rules. Build the template as WordPress block patterns that any community can reuse. Output: approved page designs.
    Phase 3 · Build & testMar – Apr 2027
    Build pages on a staging copy, connect the calendar category and forms, run an accessibility check (WCAG 2.1 AA), test on phones, and have 5–8 parishioners of different ages try it. Output: a site ready to launch.
    Phase 4 · LaunchMay 2027
    Go live, set up redirects from the old address, announce through parish bulletins, The Southern Cross, and social media, and train editors. Output: live site and editor guide.
    Phase 5 · Template kitJun – Sep 2027
    Package the “Community Page Kit”: patterns, a content checklist, a photo consent form, and a governance guide. Pilot it with 2–3 other communities (for example Vietnamese, Chamorro, or Korean) with the Office for Ethnic & Intercultural Communities. Output: 3–4 community sections on one model.
    Phase 6 · ProposalOct – Dec 2027
    Present results (traffic, downloads, sign-ups, and staff time saved) to Communications and the Chancery with a proposal to extend the design system to the whole site. That proposal becomes Plan B on proven ground.

    Team & Effort

    • DCFC web lead: 4–6 hrs/week during the build, 2 hrs/week after
    • 2–3 content editors: 2–3 hrs/week during content, then monthly
    • Diocesan staff: approvals and publishing, roughly 1–2 hrs/week
    • Optional vendor or freelancer for template work: 20–60 hours

    Budget (Planning Range)

    • Hosting: $0 (existing diocesan site)
    • Template or theme work: $0 if volunteer; roughly $2,000–$6,000 if contracted
    • Photography and video: $0–$1,500
    • Translation: volunteer, or about $500–$1,500 for professional review
    • Total: about $0–$9,000, fundable through DCFC events or a sponsor
    Section 6 · Plan B

    Top-Down: Modernize the Whole Diocesan Site

    One coordinated redesign of sdcatholic.org, with DCFC at the table

    ApproachDCFC, through the Vicar for Ethnic & Intercultural Communities, proposes that the diocese redesign sdcatholic.org as a whole. The Chancery and Communications lead it with a hired agency. Offices, parishes, schools, and all 18 cultural communities are redesigned together on a shared design system, in English and Spanish. DCFC serves as one stakeholder and an early pilot.

    Phases & Timeline

    Phase 0 · Sponsorship2–4 months
    Build the case and win approval from the Bishop, Chancellor, CFO, and Communications Director; confirm budget and Chapter 11 spending approval; form a steering committee. Output: approved charter and budget.
    Phase 1 · Audit & RFP2–4 months
    Inventory all pages, documents, and plugins, often thousands of items. Interview offices, parishes, and communities. Write a request for proposals, compare 3–5 agencies, and check references from other dioceses. Output: selected vendor and contract.
    Phase 2 · Strategy & design3–4 months
    Design the site structure, navigation, design system, and accessibility standard; plan the parish and school directory, events, giving, and Spanish workflow. Output: approved designs and a content model.
    Phase 3 · Build & migrate4–8 months
    Build the theme and integrations (giving, calendar, personnel search, and Protect & Report), migrate and clean up content, train office editors, and test accessibility. Output: new site on staging.
    Phase 4 · Launch & support1–2 months, then ongoing
    Go live, map redirects for every old address, watch search traffic, and set up an ongoing support contract and governance. Output: modernized diocesan site.

    Team & Effort

    • Steering committee: Chancery, Communications, IT, Finance, and the Vicar for Ethnic & Intercultural Communities
    • A project manager, from the diocese or the agency
    • An agency team for strategy, design, development, and QA
    • A content owner in every office and community, including DCFC

    Budget (Planning Range)

    • Agency redesign for a large diocese: often $40,000–$150,000+
    • Managed hosting and support: about $3,000–$15,000 per year
    • Staff time across offices for content and approvals: significant
    • Total: $45,000–$165,000+ over 12–24 months; quotes required

    What DCFC Would Contribute

    Requirements

    A written list of what cultural communities need: calendars, directories, resources, multilingual pages, and youth content.

    Pilot content

    The Filipino section’s content, ready to migrate, as the first community section on the new site.

    User testing

    Parishioners of every generation to test designs, including elders and newcomers.

    Section 7

    Plan A vs. Plan B: Pros, Cons & Feasibility

    Which path is better, and which one can we actually do?

    Plan A Bottom-up pilot

    Pros
    • DCFC can start now, with no large budget
    • Visible results within about 6–9 months
    • Small scope, so it is easy to approve and lower risk
    • Creates a tested model and evidence for the diocese
    • DCFC sets priorities for Filipino families
    • Builds volunteer web skills inside the community
    Cons
    • Limited by the current diocesan theme and access
    • The rest of the site stays dated around our section
    • Depends on a few volunteers, so continuity is a risk
    • Other communities may not adopt the template
    • Some work may be redone if the diocese later redesigns

    Plan B Top-down redesign

    Pros
    • Consistent, modern experience across the whole diocese
    • Fixes shared problems (navigation, accessibility, search) once
    • Professional build, paid support, clear accountability
    • All 18 communities benefit at the same time
    • Stronger security and long-term maintainability
    Cons
    • Needs Bishop and Chancery approval and a large budget
    • Hard to fund during Chapter 11 reorganization
    • 12–24 months before anything changes for families
    • DCFC is one voice among many offices
    • Large projects stall when leaders or budgets change
    • Outside DCFC’s authority to start

Plan A delivers value now and is the most credible way to make Plan B happen: a working pilot, a reusable template, and real usage numbers turn a large request into an easy next step. Switch to Plan B if discovery finds a diocesan redesign already approved or underway. In that case, DCFC joins as the pilot community and brings the Plan A content and requirements with it.
    Section 8

    Important Considerations

    Standards that apply under either plan

    Accessibility

    • Target WCAG 2.1 Level AA: alt text on every image, captions on video, strong color contrast, keyboard navigation, and labeled form fields
    • Large, readable type and simple navigation for older parishioners
    • Accessible PDFs (tagged, with real text, not scanned images), or an HTML version of key documents
    • Test with a screen reader and an automated checker before launch

    Language & Culture

    • English first, with Spanish through the diocesan workflow
    • Tagalog (and, where possible, Cebuano or Ilocano) summaries of key pages: welcome, mission, events, contact
    • Respectful use of Filipino devotions, names, and images, with the Spiritual Advisor’s review
    • Use “San Pedro Calungsod” and other naming conventions consistently

    Child Protection & Privacy

    • Written photo and video consent; parent or guardian consent for minors; no full names of minors next to photos
    • Follow diocesan Safe Environment and Protect & Report policies; link to them from volunteer pages
    • Collect only what each form needs. No birthdates, sacramental records, or financial data through web forms.
    • Publish a privacy note on every form saying who sees the data and how long it is kept

    Brand & Content Quality

    • Follow diocesan brand standards; place the DCFC logo next to the diocesan identity, not in place of it
    • A short style guide: voice, photo style, and event-listing format
    • Every page has an owner and a “last reviewed” date
    • Copyright: only use music, liturgical texts, and images we have rights to; credit photographers

    Sustainability & Continuity

    • At least two trained editors at all times, so no single point of failure
    • Logins in a shared password manager owned by the organization; two-factor login required
    • Plan for board transitions: hand over the site when officers change
    • An annual review of the site, plugins, and resources each fall before Simbang Gabi

    Measuring Success

    MeasureYear-one target
    Event calendar always current100% of DCFC events listed 3+ weeks ahead
    Resource downloads200+ per year
    Newsletter or volunteer sign-ups100+
    Contact repliesWithin 3 business days
    Accessibility checkNo critical issues
    Communities using the template3+ by end of 2027

    Key Risks

    • No diocesan access: use Hosting Option 3 as a temporary bridge
    • Volunteer burnout: keep the scope small; share the work across committees
    • Stale content: follow the content calendar and the sunset rule
